Public Wi-Fi Reality Check

What the coffee-shop Wi-Fi (and the apps on your phone) can see, in 5 quick stops. Plus a 2-minute fix.

1Sealed envelopes, visible addresses

Most sites use HTTPS, so the Wi-Fi owner can't read what you send. But they can still see where you're going.

TO: instagram.com · 2:14 PM · 3 min "hey did you see the game last night lol, sending you that pic now…"

Wi-Fi can see

  • Which sites and apps (domain names)
  • When and how long
  • How much data
  • Your device

Can't see (HTTPS)

  • Your messages
  • Passwords
  • Which exact page or video
  • What you typed

Analogy: the mailroom can't open your envelopes, but it writes down every address you mail to.

4Check your own phone

Want proof from your phone, not a demo?

App Privacy Report: Settings → Privacy & Security → App Privacy Report → turn on. Use your phone for a day, then open it to see every domain each app contacted.

Privacy dashboard: Settings → Security & privacy → Privacy dashboard shows which apps used your location, camera, and mic, and when. (Menu names vary by brand.)

Analogy: it's your phone's security-camera footage of every app that left the house and where it went.

5The 2-minute fix

Analogy: you can't control the coffee shop's mailroom, but you can use a P.O. box (VPN), wear a disguise (private address), and stop handing your mail to strangers (auto-join off).

Keep it legal: only look at traffic from your own devices, or a friend's phone with their OK. Capturing other people's traffic on public Wi-Fi counts as wiretapping. My Pi-hole demo uses my own phone over my own Tailscale network.
